CHAPTER 2 · LESSON 1

Types and Proper Classification of Waste

Learn how to identify, classify, and properly separate waste to help protect people and the environment.

Start Learning ↓

🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define waste segregation.
  • Identify the different types of waste.
  • Classify waste according to its characteristics.
  • Explain how to separate waste properly.

1. What Is Waste Segregation?

Waste segregation is the process of separating waste into different categories based on its type, composition, and characteristics. It helps ensure that waste is handled, recycled, treated, or disposed of using the appropriate method.

Example: Food scraps should be separated from plastic bottles, while batteries should be kept separate from ordinary household waste. Proper segregation begins where waste is generated, such as homes, schools, offices, and public places.

2. Types of Waste

Waste can be classified into four major categories.

🍂

A. Biodegradable Waste

Biodegradable waste consists of materials that microorganisms can break down naturally over time.

Examples:
  • Fruit and vegetable peels
  • Leftover food
  • Dry leaves and grass
  • Garden waste
Proper management: Suitable organic waste can often be composted to produce nutrient-rich material for soil.
♻️

B. Recyclable Waste

Recyclable waste consists of materials that can be collected and processed into new products.

Examples:
  • Paper and cardboard
  • Accepted plastic bottles
  • Aluminum cans
  • Glass containers
Proper management: Keep recyclables separate, clean when appropriate, and dry. Follow local recycling rules.
🗑️

C. Residual Waste

Residual waste cannot readily be composted or recycled through available local systems.

Examples:
  • Used tissues
  • Certain multilayer food wrappers
  • Contaminated non-recyclable packaging
  • Used napkins
Proper management: Place residual waste in the designated bin and follow local disposal guidelines.
⚠️

D. Hazardous Waste

Hazardous waste contains substances or has properties that can harm people or the environment.

Examples:
  • Used batteries
  • Certain chemical containers
  • Pesticide residues
  • Fluorescent lamps
Proper management: Keep hazardous waste separate. Do not burn, break, or pour chemicals down drains. Use authorized collection or disposal services.
Note: Local waste-management systems may include additional categories, such as special waste and electronic waste. Follow your school's or local government's classification system.

3. Proper Ways to Segregate Waste

1
Identify the waste.
Determine whether it is biodegradable, recyclable, residual, or hazardous.
2
Prepare separate containers.
Use clearly labeled bins for the categories accepted by your school or community.
3
Separate waste at the source.
Do not mix food scraps with recyclables or hazardous materials with ordinary waste.
4
Handle materials safely.
Avoid touching sharp, leaking, or potentially hazardous items directly.
5
Dispose of each category properly.
Compost suitable organic waste, send accepted recyclables to appropriate facilities, and use designated collection services for hazardous waste.

4. Why Is Correct Classification Important?

🌎

Prevents Pollution

Separating waste helps prevent contamination and reduces the risk of harmful substances reaching soil or water.

🔄

Supports Recycling

Proper sorting keeps recyclable materials suitable for collection and processing.

🚛

Improves Collection

Correct classification helps waste handlers identify materials for recycling, composting, treatment, or disposal.

Food residue on paper or cardboard can make recycling more difficult. Separating hazardous waste also reduces the risk of harmful substances entering the soil or water.

5. Common Waste Segregation Mistakes

❌ Mixing food scraps with recyclable paper.

✅ Keep food waste separate from paper and cardboard.

❌ Putting batteries in ordinary trash.

✅ Keep batteries separate and use authorized collection services.

❌ Assuming every plastic item is recyclable.

✅ Check which plastics local facilities accept.

❌ Throwing waste into an unlabeled bin.

✅ Check the category and use the correct labeled container.

❌ Leaving recyclables contaminated with food or liquids.

✅ Prepare recyclables according to local recycling rules.

🌱 Lesson Summary

Waste segregation means separating waste according to its characteristics. The four main categories are biodegradable, recyclable, residual, and hazardous waste. Identifying each category and following the proper handling method helps prevent contamination and supports responsible waste management.

Segregate Today, Sustain Tomorrow.

Small actions at home, school, and in the community can help create a cleaner environment.
